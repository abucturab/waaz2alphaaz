# Dawoodi Bohra Community

> Summary: The Dawoodi Bohra are a Shia Ismaili Tayyibi Muslim community of primarily Gujarati origin, the sole native-speaker community of Lisan-ud-Dawat, numbering approximately 1–2 million worldwide.

## Overview

The Dawoodi Bohra (also spelled Dawoodī Bohras, or simply "Bohras") are a sub-sect of the Tayyibi Ismaili branch of Shia Islam. They trace their religious lineage to the Fatimid Caliphate (909–1171 CE) of North Africa through a chain of *Dai al-Mutlaq* (absolute missionaries/viceroys). The word "Bohra" is thought to derive from the Gujarati word for "trader" (*vohorvu* — to trade), reflecting the community's historic mercantile identity.

The Dawoodi Bohra are the primary and effectively only native-speaking community of Lisan-ud-Dawat. The language is central to their religious identity — it is the medium of the *waaz* (sermon), of religious literature, of prayer supplements, and of community discourse at the institutional level.

The community is led by the **Dai al-Mutlaq** (the absolute representative of the Hidden Imam), a hereditary office held by the Syedna family. The current (54th) Dai al-Mutlaq is **Syedna Mufaddal Saifuddin** (b. 1946), based in Mumbai. The Da'wa headquarters (Al-Jamea-tus-Saifiyah) is in Surat, Gujarat.

## Key Properties

- **Religious classification**: Shia Ismaili Tayyibi (Mustalian branch); distinct from Nizari Ismailis (Aga Khani)
- **Origin geography**: Gujarat, India (Surat, Vadodara, Ahmedabad, Udaipur)
- **Estimated global population**: 1–2 million <!-- unverified; community does not release official census figures -->
- **Primary language**: Lisan-ud-Dawat (community/religious language); Gujarati, English, Urdu, and local languages also used
- **Economic profile**: Historically and presently mercantile/trading; strong professional class in diaspora
- **Organizational structure**: Highly centralized around the Dai al-Mutlaq and the Da'wa hierarchy
- **Distinctive practices**: *Ashara Mubaraka* (ten days of mourning in Muharram featuring intensive daily waaz), *misaq* (pledge of allegiance), distinctive dress code (white taqiyah cap for men, *rida* for women)

## Religious Calendar and Language Use

The Dawoodi Bohra religious calendar structures language use in important ways for this project:

**Ashara Mubaraka** (10 days of Muharram): The most important religious period. The Dai al-Mutlaq or senior *mazoon/mukasir* delivers a *waaz* (sermon) each day. These waaz are attended in person and broadcast/streamed globally. They are the highest-register, most widely-distributed LuD audio content in existence. The waaz of the Dai al-Mutlaq during Ashara is central to the waaz2alphaaz project's target domain.

**Fatemi months**: Additional waaz and religious gatherings occur throughout the year at mosques (masjids) and community gathering halls (jamaat khanas).

**Life-cycle events**: Weddings, funerals, and rites of passage involve recitation in LuD.

## The Waaz Tradition (Relevance to waaz2alphaaz)

The **waaz** (sermon) is the primary audio-linguistic product of the Dawoodi Bohra religious tradition. Key characteristics:

1. **Delivered by**: The Dai al-Mutlaq or designated deputies (*mazoon*, *mukasir*, local *amils*)
2. **Length**: Typically 1–3 hours per sitting
3. **Structure**: Opening salutations (Arabic) → Quranic recitation (Arabic) → Main discourse (LuD, with embedded Arabic, Persian, and sometimes Gujarati) → Closing duas (Arabic)
4. **Language register**: The highest register of LuD, with dense Arabic and Persian embeddings
5. **Audio availability**: Historically distributed on cassette tapes, then CDs; now live-streamed and available via community apps/websites
6. **Transcription**: Some waaz have published transcripts in the LuD script; many do not

The Ashara Mubaraka waaz of the Dai al-Mutlaq, delivered over 10 days in Muharram, are the most prestigious and most widely available content. These are the primary target for the waaz2alphaaz project.

## Interfaces & Dependencies

From a data and NLP perspective:
- **Al-Jamea-tus-Saifiyah** (Bohra Arabic/religious university in Surat and Karachi): The main institution producing scholars trained in LuD; has internal manuscript resources
- **Dawat-e-Hadiyah** (Da'wa administrative body): Controls official publication and broadcast of waaz content
- **FatemiMasjid.org / Dawat websites**: Online distribution of waaz audio; primary public source of LuD audio content <!-- unverified: exact URLs may have changed -->
- **Community apps**: Bohra community apps for diaspora members often host waaz recordings

## Known Behaviors & Gotchas

- **Access restrictions**: Some waaz content is restricted to community members (*mumineen*) and requires login/membership verification. This limits availability of data for AI training.
- **Speaker variation**: The Dai al-Mutlaq's waaz accent and style differs from local *amil* waaz; models trained on one may not generalize well.
- **Ashara acoustics**: Ashara waaz are delivered in large venues (often converted exhibition halls or large mosque complexes) with thousands of attendees; ambient noise, echo, and audience responses (weeping, recitation call-and-response) are acoustically present in recordings.
- **Code-switching frequency**: In any given waaz sentence, the speaker may shift between LuD, Quranic Arabic, Persian poetry, and sometimes English (in diaspora settings). ASR must handle all.

## Current Status

The Dawoodi Bohra community is active, growing, and increasingly digitally engaged. The 54th Dai's waaz are professionally recorded and streamed. Community-facing digital infrastructure is well-developed. However, formal NLP/linguistic resources remain scarce and largely inaccessible outside the community.
